About Me

I am an M.S. student in Electrical Engineering at Tsinghua University, following an M.S.E. in Biomedical Engineering at Johns Hopkins University. I previously studied Computer Science at the University of Oxford and earned dual B.Eng. degrees from Xi'an Jiaotong University. This interdisciplinary training has given me a strong foundation in mathematics, machine learning, and biomedical engineering.

My research focuses on reliable modeling of clinical processes and outcomes. At Johns Hopkins, I developed SurgTTE for uncertainty-aware surgical workflow anticipation. At Oxford, I developed SurvUnc, a model-agnostic uncertainty quantification framework for survival analysis published at KDD 2025.

I am broadly interested in multimodal medical foundation models and agentic AI for healthcare, with the goal of building clinically grounded systems that combine multimodal reasoning, tool use, uncertainty awareness, and human oversight for trustworthy clinical workflows.

Open to opportunities. I am actively seeking PhD opportunities, research collaborations, and internship or full-time roles in AI research and engineering, particularly in multimodal medical AI, foundation models, and agentic systems.

A little more about me. I am an explorer—energetic, endlessly curious, and always excited to discover new ideas, places, and experiences. Outside research, I enjoy hiking, playing the piano, performing in bands and a cappella groups, and immersing myself in musical theatre.

Research Overview

"I develop clinically grounded multimodal and agentic AI systems for trustworthy healthcare workflows."

Multimodal Medical Foundation Models

Learning from medical images, video, text, and longitudinal records to support grounded clinical reasoning and robust generalization.

Surgical Workflow Understanding

Modeling surgical procedures across time to recognize ongoing activities, anticipate workflow transitions, and quantify predictive uncertainty.

Trustworthy Agentic Systems for Healthcare

Building agents that retrieve evidence, use specialized tools, communicate uncertainty, and collaborate with clinicians in auditable workflows.
